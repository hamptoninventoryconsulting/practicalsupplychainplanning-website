Safety Stock Simulator

A 52-week teaching year: forecast, uncertainty, replenishment, then the consequences for service, turns, and cash.

Teaching tool only

This is a teaching tool only. It is not suitable for real operational use, and it is not a planner or an optimiser.

The simulation runs in this browser. Nothing is stored on a server. Temporary choices stay in sessionStorage for this tab and clear when the tab closes.

Teaching prices are fixed at a $100 sell price, a $70 cost, and $30 gross profit per unit. They cannot be edited.

The Mode section is next, before the settings. Choose One year or Monte Carlo, then press the matching button. Nothing is drawn until you do. Changing a setting clears the last results. Switching to Monte Carlo does not start the fifty years.

Mode

The chart, weekly table, and Monte Carlo summary stay empty until you press the button for this mode. Changing a setting clears them.

Base demand pattern

Forecast demand is the base demand. Variability and the shock are applied on top. The pattern itself is not a weekly editor.

Replenishment

An order is placed only when calculated stock on hand is strictly below safety stock. Calculated stock is beginning stock, plus supply already due this week, minus this week's base forecast demand. Ending stock subtracts simulated demand. Landing on the safety-stock line does not order. With safety stock at zero, the line is zero. The order is one lot unless that lot would not lift calculated stock above zero, in which case it is two lots. That rule looks at calculated stock against zero, not at whether last week ended negative, and it does not keep ordering until safety stock is rebuilt.

Fixed lots are a whole number from 1 to 1000. Fixed safety stock is a whole number from 0 to 1000. Weeks cover sums the first 2, 4, 6, or 10 weeks of base demand. Starting stock is week-1 base demand plus that nominal lot, with no delivery-quantity noise.

Formula safety stock is round(Z × σD × √L). Z is 1.282, 1.645, 2.054, or 2.326 at 90%, 95%, 98%, and 99%. σD is the population standard deviation of this year's demand after the demand factor and before any shock. L is the base lead time: short 2, medium 5, or long 10. The result is constant across the 52 weeks. Monte Carlo keeps that same quantity for every year in the batch. The base lead time is not added onto the arrival week. It is used for √L and for the maximum extra delay when lead-time variability is on.

Variability

Each lever is independent. Off means no noise. Small, medium, and large are the only levels.

Demand multiplies base demand by a uniform factor, then rounds: small 0.90 to 1.10, medium 0.70 to 1.30, large 0.50 to 1.50. Off uses the rounded forecast.

Lead time adds a whole-number delay from 0 up to a maximum that depends on the base lead time. Short: 1 / 1 / 2. Medium: 1 / 2 / 3. Long: 1 / 3 / 5 (small / medium / large). The order arrives in the requirement week plus that delay. A receipt dated after week 52 does not arrive in this year. With lead-time variability off, the delay is zero.

Delivery quantity only reduces a planned lot. It does not touch starting stock. A fixed lot can lose up to 5%, 10%, or 15%, drawn as a whole number of units. A weeks-cover lot can lose weeks: 2 → 0 / 0 / −1, 4 → 0 / −1 / −2, 6 → −1 / −2 / −3, 10 → −1 / −3 / −4 (small / medium / large). The receipt is the sum of that many opening weeks of base demand.

Demand shock

When the shock is on, three weeks from week 21 through week 52 are chosen again for every year. Successive chosen weeks are at least four weeks apart. Those weeks' demand, after the usual demand factor, is doubled and rounded. You do not pick the weeks.

Inventory turns use on-hand stock only: each week's ending stock counts as zero when it is negative, then the year's simulated demand is divided by that average. A stockout does not improve turns. If the average is zero, turns are not applicable. Working capital is different on purpose: it averages ending stock with the negatives still in, then multiplies by the $70 cost. Gross profit is simulated demand times $30 and does not change because of backorders.

High turns with weak service is a poor outcome. The warning appears when turns are at least 8 and either customer service is below 95% or three or more weeks end below zero. Out-of-stock means ending stock is below zero. Ending on zero is not a stockout. Customer service is the share of the 52 weeks that are not out of stock.
